# Stamped! for Michigan State University

## Concept Summary
`Stamped! for Michigan State University` is a campus exploration app concept adapted from `Stamped! A City Passport`.

Instead of gamifying architectural discovery across cities, this version would gamify freshman orientation and campus literacy across the MSU campus. The product would help new students learn where key places are, why they matter, and how to navigate the university with more confidence.

## Core Idea
The current app loop already maps well to campus onboarding:

- cities become campus zones or districts
- landmarks become campus buildings, dining halls, parking lots, libraries, residence halls, student services, and event spaces
- passport stamps become campus completion badges
- quizzes become orientation challenges
- discovery progress becomes campus familiarity progress

This creates a version of orientation that is more interactive, self-directed, and memorable than static maps or PDF packets.

## Problem
New students often arrive on campus with low spatial familiarity and little confidence navigating:

- where their classes are
- where to eat
- where to park
- where student support offices are
- how to distinguish important buildings from one another
- how campus zones relate to daily routines

Traditional orientation materials often explain campus, but they do not create repeated engagement with the physical environment.

## Proposed Solution
Create a Michigan State-specific version of `Stamped!` that turns campus discovery into a guided challenge system.

Students would:

- browse important campus destinations
- learn what each place is for
- complete check-ins or visit goals
- answer short campus literacy quizzes
- unlock stamps or badges for campus categories
- track progress across orientation pathways

## Example Campus Categories
- Academic buildings
- Dining halls and cafeterias
- Parking lots and transit stops
- Residence halls
- Libraries and study spaces
- Student services
- Recreation and wellness spaces
- Landmarks and traditions
- Safety resources
- Event venues

## Target Users
- First-year undergraduate students
- Transfer students
- International students
- Parents during orientation support flows
- Student ambassadors and orientation leaders

## Why This Format Works
The current product structure is already strong for campus adaptation because it combines:

- visual discovery
- progress tracking
- short-form education
- completion rewards
- accessibility settings
- mobile-first browsing

That combination makes the experience useful both before arrival and during the first weeks on campus.

## Student Value
- Faster campus familiarity
- Lower navigation anxiety
- Stronger early belonging
- Better awareness of key student resources
- More engagement with campus beyond academic buildings
- More memorable orientation experience

## Institutional Value
- Stronger orientation experience without requiring every interaction to be staff-led
- Better visibility for underused but important campus resources
- More consistent communication of campus services
- Potentially stronger student confidence in the first semester
- Opportunity for co-branded digital engagement during recruitment and onboarding

## Product Structure

### 1. Campus List
Students browse campus locations by category, district, or student need.

Examples:
- Need food now
- Need parking near class
- Need academic advising
- Need a quiet place to study

### 2. Location Detail
Each location page would include:

- name
- purpose
- quick facts
- hours or availability if provided
- accessibility notes
- what students use it for
- how it connects to campus life
- optional “visit this place” challenge

### 3. Campus Quiz
Short quizzes would reinforce campus knowledge:

- Which building houses advising?
- Which lot is closest to a specific area?
- Where can students get help after hours?
- Which dining hall is in a given district?

### 4. Passport / Badge System
Students earn progress marks for:

- exploring a category
- visiting a set of places
- completing orientation tracks
- finishing resource literacy quizzes

## Example MSU Adaptation Paths

### Freshman Arrival Track
- Find your residence hall
- Locate your dining hall
- Locate your academic advising office
- Learn one library, one student support office, and one recreation space

### First Week Survival Track
- Find your first class building
- Find parking or transit access
- Find late-day dining
- Find tutoring or academic support

### Spartan Traditions Track
- Explore iconic places
- Learn campus history
- Unlock tradition-based stamps

## Recommended Feature Set for a Pilot
For an early pilot, keep the scope tight:

- 20 to 40 important campus destinations
- 5 to 8 categories
- basic progress tracking
- quiz module
- passport completion badges
- optional check-in or visit confirmation
- admin-managed content source

## Content Requirements
The university adaptation would need curated campus data:

- destination names
- short descriptions
- category tags
- hours if relevant
- location coordinates
- accessibility notes
- thumbnail or hero images
- approved terminology

## Branding Guidance
This concept should be presented as:

- `Stamped! for Michigan State University`
- `A campus exploration concept built on the Stamped! framework`

Important:
- Do not present it as officially affiliated unless MSU approves it.
- Any final branded version would need review for naming, marks, color usage, and messaging.

## Design Translation
The current app’s travel/passport identity maps well to campus:

- journey log becomes orientation progress
- architectural discovery becomes campus familiarity
- stamps become completion badges
- city hero becomes campus destination hero
- travel info becomes student utility info

The visual system should be adapted to feel more campus-specific while keeping the strongest product metaphors:

- progress
- collection
- exploration
- reward

## Suggested Pitch Narrative
When showing this to MSU, lead with the problem and student outcome rather than the app itself:

1. New students struggle to learn campus quickly.
2. Orientation materials are informative but not always engaging.
3. `Stamped!` turns campus familiarity into an interactive exploration system.
4. The result is a more confident, informed, and connected student experience.

## What Makes the Concept Credible
- A working app framework already exists
- The interaction model is already proven inside the current product
- The adaptation does not require inventing an entirely new app category
- The concept can start small as a pilot and expand later

## What Would Strengthen the Pitch Further
- MSU-specific mock screens
- a short pilot rollout plan
- an estimate of initial content needs
- a one-page adoption proposal
- one visual showing the app translated from city exploration to campus navigation
